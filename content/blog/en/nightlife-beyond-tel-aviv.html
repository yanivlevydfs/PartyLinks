Tel Aviv gets most of the attention, but Israeli nightlife doesn't stop at the city limits. Jerusalem and Haifa offer scenes with a completely different character, sometimes more intimate, sometimes more surprising, and almost always cheaper.

Jerusalem

Night in Jerusalem starts at the market. Once the stalls close, the alleys of Mahane Yehuda fill with bars, music and people, creating one of the most unique nightlife spots in the country. The city center adds bars, small clubs and live music venues. The crowd is diverse: students, artists and visitors from all over the world.

Keep Shabbat in mind: in Jerusalem a large share of venues close on Friday night for the Jewish day of rest, so going out shifts to Thursday and Saturday night after Shabbat ends.

Haifa

Haifa is a city of neighborhoods on the mountain, and its nightlife is concentrated mostly down below. Downtown, with its old buildings and warehouses near the port, has become a hub for bars, parties and cultural spaces. The German Colony offers a calmer evening, with restaurants and bars along the boulevard. Here too the crowd is mixed, and some of the events come from the city's large student community.

Why go out of town

  • Prices. Entry and drinks are usually cheaper than in Tel Aviv.
  • Atmosphere. Smaller venues, a regular crowd and a sense of community.
  • Shows. Many artists tour nationally, and tickets outside Tel Aviv are sometimes easier to get.
