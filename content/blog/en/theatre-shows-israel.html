A night at the theatre is a calm, rewarding way to go out: no need to stay up until morning, and plenty to talk about on the way home. But there's a lot on offer, and if you don't go often it's not always clear where to start.

How to choose a show

  • Comedy: a safe pick for a night with friends or a date. Easy to enjoy even if you're not a regular theatregoer.
  • Drama: classic plays and original Israeli work. Read the synopsis beforehand so you know what you're in for.
  • Musicals: singing, dancing and big productions. Great for families and music lovers. The best seats sell out fast.
  • Fringe and small theatre: small halls, experimental productions, cheaper tickets and a close-up view of the actors.

Subscriptions and tips

  • Subscriptions: most of the major theatres offer subscriptions covering several shows a season. If you go at least three or four times a year, it's worth checking.
  • Seats: in a large hall, a middle row is usually better than the front row. In a small theatre almost every seat is good.
  • Arrive early. Once the show starts, latecomers are sometimes not let in until the intermission.
  • Language: some shows are performed with subtitles in other languages. This is noted on the show page.
