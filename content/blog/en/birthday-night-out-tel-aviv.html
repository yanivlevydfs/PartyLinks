A birthday is a great reason to go out. But a night at a club with a group of friends takes a bit more planning than just heading out. You need to find a party everyone will enjoy, make sure everyone gets in, decide whether to book a table, and make sure everyone gets home safely. This guide walks through every step, so on the night itself you can just celebrate.

Pick a party that fits the group's taste

The most common mistake is choosing a party based on what the birthday person likes, then finding out half the friends aren't into it. A mixed group needs music that's easy to get into.

  • Mixed group: Mainstream and pop parties, with hits everyone knows, are the safe bet.
  • Friends who love to sing along: Mizrahi parties and Israeli music are almost a guarantee of a happy night. We have a guide to Mizrahi shows and parties.
  • A group that wants to dance until morning: House or techno parties. Just make sure everyone is really up for it, because these are long nights.
  • Hip hop and R&B: For a group that loves that sound, there's a guide to hip hop and R&B parties.

A simple tip: send the group two or three event links and let them vote. That way everyone feels part of the decision.

Group tickets

When the group is big, tickets are the first thing to lock in. Popular weekend parties sometimes sell out in advance, and nothing is more of a downer than a friend stuck outside on your birthday.

  • Buy early and together. One person buys for everyone and each friend pays them back. It also guarantees everyone gets the same price tier.
  • Ask about group prices. Some parties offer a group discount or package. Check the event page or ask the organizers.
  • Watch the age limit. If some friends in the group are younger, check the party's age limit in advance.

More ways to pay less are in our guide to saving on tickets, and in the guide to free-entry events.

Table and bottle service: when is it worth it?

At many clubs you can book a table for a group, usually with a commitment to a bottle or a minimum spend. It's not right for every night, but on a birthday it has a few advantages: you have a home base to come back to, somewhere to put your things, and sometimes faster entry.

Before booking, find out a few things: how many people the table covers, what exactly is included in the price, whether guests also need an entry ticket, and how late you can arrive and still keep the table. And one especially important question: can you bring a cake or ask for sparklers on the bottle? Many places are happy to help you celebrate, but it needs to be arranged in advance.

If the budget is tight, there's no need for a table. A group that arrives together and claims a corner by the dance floor can celebrate with exactly the same enthusiasm.

Timing: when to arrive and what to do before

At Tel Aviv clubs the dance floor usually fills up after midnight. That leaves time for dinner or a bar beforehand, which is exactly the place to raise a toast, open presents and take the photos everyone wants. Pick a bar close to the club so the walk over is short.

If entry is free or cheaper until a certain time, it's worth planning the night around that. And if it's some of your friends' first time at a club, send them our guide to your first club night.

Budget: how much to plan for

A birthday night adds up fast. Think ahead about every part of it: entry ticket, food or drinks beforehand, drinks at the club, and the ride home. Agree with the group in advance on how you're splitting costs, especially if you want to cover the birthday person's share. Expense-splitting apps save a lot of awkwardness at the end of the night.

Getting home safely

  • Plan the way home before you go out. On weekends public transport is limited (most of it stops for Shabbat, from Friday afternoon to Saturday night), and in the small hours taxis are slow to arrive.
  • Designated driver or a shared taxi. Whoever drinks doesn't drive. Period.
  • No one gets left alone. Agree in advance that everyone leaves together, or that each person updates the group chat when they get home.
  • Keep your phone charged. A small power bank in your pocket can save the end of the night.
