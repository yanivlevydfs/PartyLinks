Israel's techno scene is one of the most active in the Eastern Mediterranean. Every weekend there are parties in clubs, warehouses and pop-up spaces, with local DJs alongside artists from abroad. If you're just starting out, the names and styles can be confusing. This guide sorts it out.

The styles in brief

  • Melodic techno: sweeping melodies and emotional build-ups. The most accessible style if you're coming from house or pop.
  • Peak time and classic techno: a steady beat, deep bass and hypnotic repetition. This is the sound of the dance floor at peak hours.
  • Hard techno and industrial: fast, loud and tough. For people who want to dance nonstop.
  • Minimal: few elements, lots of groove. Music that reveals itself slowly.

If techno feels too hard for you, start with house parties, which are usually warmer and bouncier.

How to pick a party

The first thing to check is the lineup. A DJ you love is worth more than any marketing blurb. Every event on our site lists the lineup with a link to each artist's page, where you can see all their upcoming gigs. The second thing is the venue: a permanent club with a good sound system guarantees a solid experience, while parties in pop-up spaces promise surprises.

Dance floor rules

  • Arrive late, stay long. The most important set of the night usually starts in the late hours.
  • Protect your ears. Musician's earplugs don't lower the sound quality, only the volume. Your ears will thank you.
  • Drink water. Lots of water, all night long.
  • Respect the dance floor. Many techno parties ask you not to take photos. It's part of the culture, and it's why the dance floor feels free.
