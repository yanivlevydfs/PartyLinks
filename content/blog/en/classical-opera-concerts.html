A classical concert is unlike any other show: a quiet hall, live unamplified sound, and an orchestra or a single musician filling the space. You can come with no prior knowledge at all, and you should.

Types of concerts

  • Symphony concert: A full orchestra and large-scale works. A good starting point if you want the full experience.
  • Chamber music: A small ensemble, a smaller hall and an intimate atmosphere. You can hear each instrument on its own.
  • Recital: A single soloist, usually with piano. An evening that focuses on the artist and their instrument.
  • Opera: Singing, orchestra, sets and costumes. Usually with surtitles, so you can follow the story even without knowing the language.

Tips before you go

  • First concert? Choose a program with familiar pieces or a shorter evening. Concerts for young audiences and families are also a good way to start.
  • Arrive on time. Once a piece has started, latecomers usually aren't let into the hall until the next break.
  • Subscriptions: Orchestras and opera houses usually offer subscription series, which work out cheaper if you go several times a season.
  • What to wear: Most concerts have no strict dress code. Neat and comfortable is enough.
