Independence Day eve is the one night a year when the whole country takes to the streets at the same time. In 2027, Yom Ha'atzmaut (Israel's Independence Day) falls on Wednesday, May 12, and begins on Tuesday evening, May 11, the moment Yom HaZikaron (Memorial Day for fallen soldiers and victims of terror) ends. The sharp shift from a day of mourning to a night of celebration is part of the holiday's character, and worth keeping in mind when you make plans.

What the night looks like

  • Monday and Tuesday, May 10–11: Memorial Day. Nightlife venues are closed or quiet, and no events take place.
  • Tuesday evening, May 11: the holiday eve. Many cities have municipal stages with live concerts and fireworks, and parties at bars and clubs go on until late.
  • Wednesday, May 12: the holiday itself. It's a day for barbecues, trips and day parties, and in the evening everyone goes back to routine, since the next day is a regular Thursday.

Israeli and Mizrahi music

Independence Day is peak season for Israeli singers, and many of them perform on several stages on the same night. If you prefer a concert in a hall, with a seat and good sound, you'll find every show on the Israeli music page and the Mizrahi music page.

Tips for the holiday eve

  • Get to the municipal stages early. The free concerts draw huge crowds, and entry to the area is sometimes closed once it fills up.
  • Check traffic arrangements. Streets around the stages are closed to traffic, and public transport runs on a special schedule. Walking or cycling is easiest.
  • Buy party tickets in advance. Independence Day eve is a packed night at the clubs, and many parties sell out.
