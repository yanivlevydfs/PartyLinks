New Year's Eve (called Silvester in Israel) is the night with the highest expectations of the year, and also the night when it's easiest to be disappointed: long lines, prices that jump at the last minute and a party that doesn't suit your group. The good news for 2026 is that December 31 falls on a Thursday. In Israel that means the night kicks off the weekend, and there's no need to get up for work the next day.

Three questions before you choose

  1. What kind of music? A techno party until morning, a night of hits, Mizrahi or a live show with a countdown are completely different experiences. Decide on the style before you start scrolling.
  2. How many of you are there? A big group suits a big venue with tickets bought in advance. For a couple or three friends, a bar with a DJ can be more pleasant and less stressful.
  3. How are you getting home? Midnight on New Year's Eve is one of the busiest moments of the year for rides. A place close to home, or to friends you can crash with, is worth a lot.

When to buy a ticket

Most big parties sell tickets in release tiers: the first is cheap, and each tier that sells out pushes the price up. On New Year's Eve the gap between the first and last tier can be significant. So if you've already decided on a party, there's no reason to wait.
