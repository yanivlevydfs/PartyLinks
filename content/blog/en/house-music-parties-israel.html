If there's one genre that brings together people who come to dance and people who just come to have a good time, it's house. It's warm, it's joyful, and it works just as well in a bar at 8pm as on a packed dance floor at 3am. It has a big, loyal following in Israel, and house parties happen all week long, not just on weekends. This guide will help you understand what you're hearing and pick the right party.

What exactly is house?

House was born in Chicago in the 1980s, out of disco, soul and funk. Its foundation is simple: a steady four-on-the-floor beat, a kick on every beat, deep bass, and vocals, chords or samples on top. What sets it apart from other electronic genres is the groove. House is built to get your body moving, not to stress you out or overwhelm you.

Since then house has spread around the world and branched into dozens of directions. Some are soft and melodic, some are very close to techno, and some draw on completely different musical worlds. That's why two parties both described as "house" can feel very different.

House subgenres

When a party is described as "house", it can mean a lot of things. Here are the styles you'll come across most often:

Deep house

Soft, atmospheric and full of warm sounds. The tempo is usually slower, the bass is round and the mood is laid-back. It's the perfect sound for sunset, a bar with a small dance floor or the early hours of the evening.

Tech house

A hybrid of house and techno. It has the groove of house, but with heavier bass, minimal sounds and high energy. It's one of the most popular styles on dance floors today, and it sounds great in a club at peak time.

Afro house

House with African rhythmic influences: percussion, vocals and a rolling, hypnotic beat. There's something ritualistic about it, and it's very danceable. If you like this sound, it's worth checking out Afro parties.

Disco house and nu disco

Back to the roots: disco samples, funky bass lines, strings and big vocals. It's the happiest, bounciest style, and it also draws people who don't necessarily see themselves as electronic music fans. You'll find more from this world at disco and funk parties.

How is house different from techno?

The two genres share the same basic beat, so they're easy to mix up. The difference is in character. House is warmer, more melodic and more human, with lots of vocals, chords and a groove that makes you dance with a smile. Techno is colder, more minimal and repetitive, building tension over time. The crowd and atmosphere differ accordingly: house parties tend to be social and relaxed, while techno parties tend to be more inward-looking and focused on the dance floor.

The line between the two isn't always sharp, and tech house is exactly where they meet. If you want to get to know the other side too, read our guide to techno parties in Israel.

When should you arrive?

It depends on the party. Bar and sunset house parties usually start early, and the best atmosphere there is in the evening. Club parties usually fill up after midnight, and the peak comes later. The most useful rule: check the lineup to see who's playing when, and arrive before the set you came for. How to choose a house party

  • By style. Read the event description. Deep house and Afro house promise a completely different night from tech house.
  • By the DJ. Listen to a set or two beforehand. It's the best way to know what's in store.
  • By the venue. A small bar suits a social evening, while a club with a good sound system suits people who came to dance. The venues page shows what's happening at each venue.
  • By the time. A sunset party, an evening at a bar or a night until morning. Each is a different experience.
