Going out without paying for entry isn't a fantasy. Every week in Israel there are free-entry events: bars with a DJ, parties that open their doors for free in the early hours, small gigs, and events that just ask you to register in advance. The problem is they're scattered across lots of sites and pages, and the terms change from event to event. This guide explains how it works and how not to miss out.

What does “free entry until 23:30” mean?

This is the most common kind of free entry. The venue doesn't charge anyone who comes in before a certain time, and after that it starts charging the regular entry price. The logic is simple: the venue wants the dance floor to fill up early, because an empty floor at the start of the night doesn't draw people in, and a full bar sells more. You get free entry, they get a crowd.

A few things worth knowing:

  • The time is when you get through the door. If you were in line at 23:20 and got in at 23:40, you'll probably pay. On busy nights the line can be long, so leave yourself a margin.
  • The night starts slowly. If you arrive early, you'll see a dance floor that's still filling up. That's fine, it's the price of free entry, and the vibe builds on its own.
  • Extra conditions. Sometimes free entry only applies with registration, only for a certain age or only on certain days. Read the description.

Pre-registration and zero-cost tickets

Many free events require registration. Sometimes it's a simple form, and sometimes it's a “ticket” on a ticketing site that costs ₪0. The point is to know how many people are coming and not exceed the venue's capacity. Once registration is full it closes, even if the event itself is still a week away.

The key tip here: if you see a free event that interests you, register right away. Registering doesn't commit you to going, but without it you might not get in. And if you end up not going, it's nice to cancel and free up a spot for someone else, if that option exists.

Guest lists

A guest list is another way to get in for free or at a discount. Promoters and organizers put names on a list, and whoever is on it gets in on better terms, usually until a certain time. To get on the list you typically need to contact the party's promoter or sign up through the link they share.

Keep in mind that guest lists have rules too: they can close early, they're sometimes valid only until a certain time, and they don't always guarantee entry if the venue is full.

Why the free ticket tier sells out first

At parties that sell tickets in tiers, the first tier is sometimes free or nearly free. Organizers use it to build momentum and fill the event early. Naturally, it's limited in number and everyone wants it, so it sells out fastest. Anyone who waits ends up facing the next tier, which costs money.

The takeaway is simple: when you see a free tier, don't wait. For more on ticket tiers, service fees and other ways to save, read our guide on how to save on party tickets.

Free parties in Tel Aviv and beyond

Tel Aviv has the biggest concentration of bars and clubs, so it also has the largest number of free events. Bars with a DJ midweek are a good place to start, because from Sunday to Wednesday (the Israeli workweek starts on Sunday) fewer places charge entry. Everything happening in the city is on the Tel Aviv page, and if you want to choose by area, try our guide to nightlife by neighborhood.

Outside Tel Aviv there are also plenty of free-entry events, mostly at bars and community events. On the map you can see what's happening near you, and there are more ideas in our guide to nightlife beyond Tel Aviv.

Checklist before you go out

  • Check whether entry is free all night or only until a certain time.
  • Check whether you need to register in advance, and register right away.
  • Arrive with time to spare before the cutoff, because of the line.
  • Bring ID, even to a free event.
  • Budget for the bar. Free entry doesn't mean a free night, and drinks are usually where the venue makes its money.
  • Be flexible. If the event you wanted is full, there's almost always another free event the same night.
  • And if you've decided at the last minute, check what's on tonight.
