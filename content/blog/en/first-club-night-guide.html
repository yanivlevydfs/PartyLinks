Your first night at a club can be exciting and a little nerve-racking. What do you wear? When do you show up? What happens at the door? Here's everything you need to know, so you can focus on the music.

Before: picking a party

Start with the music. If you like hits, a mainstream party is a safe bet. If you're curious about electronic music, house is a gentler way in than techno. Check the event page for the lineup, the opening time and the minimum age.

At the door

  • ID. Without photo ID, most places won't let you in. Even if you look older.
  • Ticket ready. Save your ticket on your phone in advance, and make sure your battery is full.
  • Door selection. Some places reserve the right to refuse entry. A small, relaxed group gets in more easily than a big, loud crowd.

What to wear

Comfortable shoes first and foremost. You'll be standing and dancing for hours. Most clubs have no formal dress code, but it's worth checking the event page. In winter, bring a layer that's easy to take off, because it gets very hot inside.

On the dance floor

  • Water. Drink water throughout the night, even if you don't feel like it.
  • Earplugs. Hours of loud music damage your hearing. Earplugs made for concerts keep the sound clear and just lower the volume.
  • Your drink. Don't leave a drink unattended, and don't accept a drink from a stranger.
  • Meeting point. Agree in advance with your friends where to meet if you get separated. In a loud venue, your phone won't always help.

After: getting home

Plan your way home before you head out. On weekends there's no full public transport (most buses and trains stop for Shabbat, from Friday afternoon to Saturday night), and late at night demand for taxis is high. Never get in a car with a driver who's been drinking. A party close to home saves a lot of hassle. On the events map it's easy to pick something nearby.
