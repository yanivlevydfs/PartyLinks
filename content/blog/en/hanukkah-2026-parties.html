Hanukkah, the Jewish festival of lights, is one of the few holidays in the calendar when eight nights in a row feel like a vacation. Schools are closed, lots of people take a few days off, and clubs, bars and concert halls put together an especially packed lineup. In 2026 the holiday starts on Friday, December 4, and ends on Saturday night, December 12, so it covers two full weekends. That's rare, and it's a chance to plan ahead.

How to plan the eight nights

The common mistake is trying to do everything on the first weekend. Our advice is simple: pick one big night each weekend, and leave midweek for lighter outings, like a bar with a DJ, an acoustic show or a stand-up night.

  • Friday, Dec 4, the first night: the first candle is lit in the evening, and many people spend this night with family. Parties start later.
  • Midweek, Dec 6-9: the best time for concerts and smaller events. Fewer crowds, and sometimes lower prices too.
  • Thursday and Friday, Dec 10-11: the second weekend is the peak of the season. If there's a party you don't want to miss, this is the time to buy a ticket in advance.
  • Saturday night, Dec 12: the last night of the holiday, and usually one of the busiest.

Live shows and stand-up

Not every holiday night has to end on a dance floor. Hanukkah is peak season for live shows: many artists add extra dates, and comedians put on additional nights too. If you're going out with a mixed group, a show is an option that works for almost everyone.

For all upcoming stand-up nights, there's a stand-up page that updates constantly.

Tips for going out smart over the holiday

  • Buy early. At most events tickets are sold in tiers, and the first tier is the cheapest. During holidays it sells out faster.
  • Look for free entry. Many places offer free entry until a certain time. Arriving early can save you the whole entry price. See our guide to saving on tickets.
  • Mind the age limit. Many holiday parties are 18+ or 21+. The minimum age is listed on every event on our site.
  • Plan your ride home. Public transport is limited on weekends (it largely stops for Shabbat), and taxi demand is high during the holiday. Sort out in advance who you're going home with and how.
