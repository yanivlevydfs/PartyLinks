Lag BaOmer is a night of bonfires, but not only that. In 2027 the holiday falls on Tuesday, May 25, and the main night is Monday evening, May 24. Thirteen days after Independence Day, it's another chance for a midweek night out, and in particular to hear live music after a period when many people stay away from it.

Why Lag BaOmer is also a night of concerts

During the counting of the Omer, between Passover and Lag BaOmer, many Israelis traditionally avoid live music and weddings. For many, Lag BaOmer marks the end of that period, so the calendar fills up: concerts, parties, and wedding season kicks back into gear. If you're looking for a show in the weeks after the holiday, you'll have plenty to choose from.

All live shows in Israel are gathered on the live shows page, and Mizrahi music (the Middle Eastern-influenced Israeli pop that headlines many holiday events) is on the Mizrahi page.

Bonfire first, then a party

A typical Lag BaOmer evening starts around a bonfire with friends or family and moves on to a bar or a party. Since the holiday falls midweek this year, most of the big parties will happen on the nearest weekend, before or after. Electronic music fans will also find the first outdoor parties of the season around this time, mostly trance.

Lag BaOmer tips

  • Check the bonfire rules. Local authorities decide where fires are allowed, and sometimes there are bans because of the weather.
  • Mind the air. Air quality in cities drops on this night because of the smoke. If you're sensitive to it, an indoor event is a better bet.
  • Tuesday is a workday. Schools are usually closed, but most workplaces run as normal. Plan your night accordingly.
