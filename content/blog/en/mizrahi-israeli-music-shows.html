Mizrahi music, the Middle Eastern and Mediterranean-flavored pop that dominates Israeli radio, and Israeli music in general are the soundtrack to most weddings, brit milah celebrations and parties in the country, and not by chance. This is the genre where everyone knows the words, that gets people standing on chairs and that brings generations together. But when you search for "Mizrahi shows" or "Israeli music shows," you quickly find there are several very different kinds of nights under the same heading. This guide helps you understand what to expect at each one and pick the right night.

Concert or party: what's the difference?

The first and most important difference is between a concert and a party. At a concert there's one artist, they're the reason you came, and the whole evening is built around them. You hear their songs, sometimes with a full band and sometimes with backing tracks and a musician or two, and the crowd sings along to every word.

At a Mizrahi party there's no single star. A DJ plays hits by many artists, from the classics to songs released last month, sometimes mixing in Israeli pop, Greek music or Mediterranean hits. The focus is on dancing and the vibe, not on any one artist. There's also a format that combines both: a DJ party where an artist comes on for a short set at the peak of the night.

Seated or standing?

This is the question most worth checking before you buy a ticket, because it changes the whole experience.

  • Seated shows: in concert halls, cultural centers and amphitheaters. There are assigned seats, the show starts more or less on time and ends at a reasonable hour. Good for an evening with your parents, a date, or if you're mainly there to listen. Most of the time people get up and dance by their seats for the big songs, so don't worry about it being too polite.
  • Standing shows: in clubs and large halls without chairs. There are no assigned spots, and whoever arrives early gets a place near the stage. The atmosphere is looser and people dance all night.

Many big shows have a mix: a standing area in front of the stage and seated stands behind, at different prices. Check the seating map on the ticketing site before you choose.

Club shows: what to expect

A club show is a completely different experience from a concert-hall show. The doors open at the time printed on the ticket, but the artist usually takes the stage much later, often after midnight. Until then a DJ warms up the crowd, and in practice that's part of the night, not just waiting.

  • The set itself is shorter. At a club show the artist usually performs for less time than at a full concert-hall show, and after they leave the stage the party carries on.
  • Arrive at a reasonable time, not too early. There's no need to be there when the doors open, but if you want a good spot near the stage, don't show up at the last minute.
  • Tables and bottles. At many club shows you can book a table with a bottle for a group. It costs more, but gives you a fixed spot to come back to. If you're celebrating something, read the guide to a birthday night out in Tel Aviv.
  • Dress code. At Mizrahi club shows people tend to dress up more than at, say, techno parties. It's not required, but you won't feel out of place if you make an effort.

Israeli music: not just Mizrahi

"Israeli music shows" covers a whole world: pop singers, rock bands, singer-songwriters performing with a guitar, community sing-along nights and tribute shows to classic artists. The format varies accordingly. A show by a big pop star looks very different from an intimate night in a small club with a songwriter telling stories between songs.

Tips before you buy a ticket

  • Buy early for big shows. Shows by in-demand artists, especially around the holidays, sometimes sell out fast. Wait until the last minute and you might miss out or pay more.
  • Check what's included. Is it a full concert, a short club show or a party with a guest performance? The description on the ticketing site usually makes it clear.
  • Watch for age limits. Club shows usually have a minimum entry age, while concert halls generally don't.
  • Look for deals. We wrote about ways to pay less in the guide to saving on tickets.
