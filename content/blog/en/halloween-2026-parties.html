In recent years Halloween has become one of the most popular nights of the autumn in Israel: clubs get decked out like haunted houses, and the dance floor fills with witches, skeletons and horror-movie characters. If you're planning to go out this year, here's everything you need to know to pick a party, nail your costume and avoid discovering at the last minute that tickets are sold out.

When is Halloween 2026?

Halloween is always on October 31, and this year the date falls on a Saturday. In practice, the main party night is Friday, October 30, when most clubs in Tel Aviv and the other big cities hold their main event. Some places keep the celebration going on Saturday night, October 31, after Shabbat ends, which is actually Halloween night itself. And some kick off the weekend as early as Thursday.

If you want to celebrate twice, you absolutely can: Friday for the big party, and Saturday night for a more relaxed evening at a bar with some atmosphere. Just make sure your costume can survive two nights.

Why Halloween tickets sell out early

Halloween is one night a year, and everyone wants to go out. Demand is packed into a few days, so popular parties sell out faster than on a regular weekend. Most parties also sell tickets in tiers, so whoever buys early pays less, and whoever waits pays more for the exact same ticket.

  • Decide early with your friends. The longest part is usually getting the group to agree. Once you've decided, buy.
  • Compare the final price. The same party can be sold on several sites with different service fees. On SPOTX you see all the options in one place.
  • Don't give up on a sold-out party. Sometimes extra tickets are released or a new tier opens. It's worth checking again closer to the date.

Halloween costume ideas

A good Halloween costume doesn't have to be expensive. It needs to be recognizable, comfortable to dance in and able to survive a whole night in the heat of a packed dance floor.

Classics that always work

  • Vampire: black clothes, red lips, a little fake blood at the corner of the mouth. Simple and effective.
  • Zombie: old clothes you don't mind ripping, gray and dark makeup around the eyes. One of the cheapest and most convincing costumes.
  • Witch: a pointy hat, a black cloak or dress, and a broom if you're willing to carry it around all night.
  • Skeleton: a shirt or bodysuit with a skeleton print and black-and-white face paint. Looks great under UV light.

Group costumes

If you're going out as a group, a shared costume always stands out: a haunted family, the cast of a well-known horror movie, or each of you as a different character from the same series. It also helps you find each other on the dance floor.

Practical tips

  • Comfort first. A full-face mask gets stifling after an hour. Makeup is better.
  • Shoes for dancing. Your costume can be scary, but your shoes should be comfortable.
  • No props that look like weapons. Many venues won't let you in with them, even if they're plastic.
  • Somewhere for your phone and keys. Costumes usually have no pockets. A small bag saves a lot of hassle.

How is Halloween different from Purim?

On paper the two holidays sound similar: you dress up and go to a party. In practice the vibe is different. On Purim anything goes, and a funny or colorful costume is exactly right. Halloween leans dark and scary: horror-movie characters, heavy makeup, fake blood and decor designed to unsettle. The music sometimes leans darker too, with plenty of techno and electronic, alongside mainstream parties and hits.

Another difference is timing. Purim usually stretches over several days of events, while Halloween is concentrated in one weekend. That makes planning ahead more important.

How to choose a Halloween party

First, choose by the music. A Halloween party with music you don't like will still be a party with music you don't like, even with the best decorations. Then check the event description: is there a dress code, a costume contest, an age limit or free entry before a certain time?
